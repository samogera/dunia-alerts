How it works

From a forecast to a safe school day.

Every 15 minutes, for every pilot school.

  1. 01

    Watch

    The dashboard reads air quality, weather and river forecasts for each school.

  2. 02

    Spot it early

    It flags danger now and up to 48 hours ahead: dirty air, heavy rain, great heat or a rising river.

  3. 03

    A person decides

    The Dunia team checks it, then sends the head teacher a short WhatsApp or SMS with one clear step.

  4. 04

    Act and learn

    The school acts, parents are told, and every response is timed and recorded for the pilot report.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

Is Dunia Alerts free for schools?

Yes. Schools never pay. The pilot is run by Dunia Endelevu and supported by donors.

Where does the data come from?

Weather forecasts from Open-Meteo, air quality from the Copernicus Atmosphere Monitoring Service, river flow from the Global Flood Awareness System, and, where available, ground sensors through OpenAQ, including AirQo sensors in Nairobi.

Does a computer send the warnings?

No. The dashboard suggests an alert when a reading or forecast crosses a safe level. A person on the Dunia team checks it and decides to send it.

Is the demo real?

No. The demo uses made-up schools and example data, so anyone can try every step safely. It is clearly marked, and nothing in it is a real reading or a real result.

Who can sign in to the live pilot?

The Dunia team running the Nairobi pilot. There is no public sign-up: the team creates each account. Anyone can try the demo without signing in. Lost your password? Write to hello@duniaendelevu.org.

What happens to children’s information?

Dunia Alerts never records children’s names. It records what the school did, how fast it replied and whether parents were told. The pilot’s records stay on the Dunia team’s devices and in their backups.

Does the classroom screen collect anything about children?

No. It shows the room’s temperature, fresh air (CO₂), dust and humidity, and the weather outside. It has no camera or microphone, shows no names and has no controls for learners.

Can another city or school network use it?

We hope so. Get in touch through Dunia Endelevu and we will share what we learn from the pilot.
