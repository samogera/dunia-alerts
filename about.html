How it works

  1. 1

    Watch

    Every 15 minutes the dashboard reads air quality, weather and river forecasts for each pilot school.

  2. 2

    Spot it early

    It flags danger now and up to 48 hours ahead: unhealthy air, heavy rain, great heat or a rising river.

  3. 3

    A person decides

    The Dunia team checks the reading, then sends the head teacher a short WhatsApp or SMS with one clear step.

  4. 4

    Act and learn

    The school acts, parents are told, and every response is timed and recorded for the pilot report.

Built to be trusted

  • A person always decides. The computer suggests; the Dunia team checks and sends.
  • Open data. Every reading says where it came from: a ground sensor, a model or the team.
  • Two languages. Every parent message is in English and Kiswahili.
  • No children’s names. Only what the school did, how fast it replied and whether parents were told.
  • Free for schools. Schools never pay. It works on any phone, with no app to install.

Quick guide for the team

  1. Add the pilot schools under Schools: name, area, pupils and the head teacher’s phone.
  2. Check “Needs attention” on the Overview each morning and afternoon.
  3. Prepare the alert. For air, compare the nearest AirQo sensor first. Forecast alerts say when the danger is expected.
  4. Message the head teacher from the School desk in one tap, then record their reply and what the school did.
  5. Tell parents from Parents, in English and Kiswahili, and mark them informed.
  6. Close the alert with its result. Impact builds the pilot report and CSV.
  7. Download a backup every week, and use Practice mode to train new people.

Questions

Is Dunia Alerts free for schools?

Yes. Schools never pay. The pilot is run by Dunia Endelevu and supported by donors.

Where does the data come from?

Weather forecasts from Open-Meteo, air quality from the Copernicus Atmosphere Monitoring Service, river flow from the Global Flood Awareness System, and, where available, ground sensors through OpenAQ, including AirQo sensors in Nairobi.

Does a computer send the warnings?

No. The dashboard suggests an alert when a reading or forecast crosses a safe level. A person on the Dunia team checks it and decides to send it.

What happens to children’s information?

Dunia Alerts never records children’s names. It records what the school did, how fast it replied and whether parents were told. The pilot’s records stay on the Dunia team’s device and in its backups.

Can another city or school network use it?

We hope so. Get in touch through Dunia Endelevu and we will share what we learn from the pilot.
